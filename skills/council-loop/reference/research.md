# The evidence behind each rule

ELI5: Practice only makes you better if someone checks your answers. Lots of ideas only help if you
can pick the good ones fairly.

| # | Finding | Evidence | Rule it created |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A strong leader with fresh-eyes experts beats one long chat | A conductor model handing sub-tasks to fresh expert instances beat standard prompting by 17.1% and multi-persona prompting by 15.2% on GPT-4 ([Meta-Prompting](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12954v1)). A lead agent plus subagents beat a single agent by 90.2% on research tasks ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)) | Chair + 7 experts in separate contexts |
| 2 | Loops beat one-shot answers, but only with real feedback | Self-Refine: ~20% absolute gain over 7 tasks ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651v2)). Reflexion: 91% vs 80% on HumanEval ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366v4)). Without outside feedback, self-correction can make answers worse ([Google DeepMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)). Giving Claude a way to verify its work improves quality 2–3x ([Cherny](https://venturebeat.com/technology/the-creator-of-claude-code-just-revealed-his-workflow-and-developers-are)) | Verify beats vote; critiques cite evidence |
| 3 | Many ideas raise the odds a good one exists; picking is the hard part | 250 samples took SWE-bench Lite from 15.9% to 56%, but voting plateaus ([Large Language Monkeys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). More calls can help, then hurt ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02419v2)). AlphaEvolve pairs many candidates with automated scorers ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)) | 7 × 13 ideas only with a rubric written first; merge duplicates |
| 4 | Groups are smart only when members think alone first | Brainstorming groups produce fewer ideas than the same people working alone ([Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/psyifp/aeechterhoff/wintersemester2011-12/seminarthemenfelderdersozialpsychologie/08_diehl_stoebe_productivityloss-brainstorming_jpsp1987.pdf)). Debating models flip right answers to wrong ones to agree ([Wynn et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v2)). Voting explains most debate gains ([Choi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17536v2)) | Isolated ideation; blind votes; change a vote only for new evidence |
| 5 | AI judges are biased by position, length and self-preference | Over 80% agreement with humans, but with position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias ([MT-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Models favour their own outputs ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)) | Blind, shuffled, equal-length cards; Borda; the chair never grades |
| 6 | Different labs catch different mistakes, if each model is strong | A jury of diverse models beat one big judge at 1/7 the cost ([PoLL](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18796v2)). Mixed models solved over 30% of ARC-AGI-2 vs 23% for one model ([Sakana AI](https://sakana.ai/ab-mcts/)). Weak models in the mix lower quality ([Self-MoA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)). [LLM Council](https://github.com/karpathy/llm-council) anonymizes peer ranking | One flagship per lab as elders; quality floors on every seat |
| 7 | Structured adversarial critique finds real problems | Debate lifted judge accuracy to 76% (models) and 88% (humans), from 48% and 60% ([Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06782v4)). Critic models beat human critiques 63% of the time, but nitpick more ([CriticGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00215v1)) | Red-team pre-mortem; S1–S3 severity; no nitpicks |
| 8 | Keep diversity while narrowing | A Pareto front beat RL by 10% with up to 35x fewer rollouts ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v1)). Islands plus critic/author roles: 95.6% vs 55.6% for best-of-N ([Mind Evolution](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09891v1)). Elo tournaments then evolution ([AI co-scientist](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18864v1)) | 7→5→3→1 with one Pareto wildcard; later loops mutate survivors |
| 9 | Spend effort where it pays; stop when done | Compute-optimal test-time scaling: >4x more efficient than best-of-N ([Snell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314v1)). Effort scaled to query complexity; ~15x tokens for multi-agent ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Loop cap of 3 in Google's ADK example ([Google](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/developers-guide-to-multi-agent-patterns-in-adk/)) | Gate with tiers; ≤3 loops; early stop |
| 10 | Compress the conversation, not the evidence | Context rot; 1–2k-token subagent summaries ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). Chain of Draft uses as little as 7.6% of the tokens ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18600)). Itemized playbook deltas avoid collapse: +10.6% ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v1)) | Word caps + ELI5; evidence stays verbatim; itemized lessons |
| 11 | Systems improve when each run teaches the next, with a human holding the pen | Plan → work → review → compound ([Every](https://every.to/guides/compound-engineering)). Versioned prompts promoted on thresholds ([OpenAI cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/partners/self_evolving_agents/autonomous_agent_retraining)). Self-improving agent: 20% → 50% on SWE-bench, sandboxed ([Darwin Gödel Machine](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)) | ≤3 lessons per run via PR; the loop never edits its own rubric or tests |
| 12 | Short instruction files; procedures in skills | CLAUDE.md under 200 lines ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). AGENTS.md as a ~100-line map ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/blog/harness-engineering)). 68% compliance at 500 instructions ([IFScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)) | ~25-line CLAUDE.md section + this skill |
| 13 | Parallelize thinking; keep doing single-threaded | Most coding is less parallel than research ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). "Writes stay single-threaded" ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)). The 16-agent compiler needed near-perfect tests ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)) | Read-only council; one writer; locked tests |
